# writing style — sami / kmab

the spec for how sentences get built. companion to
`00_READ_ME/BRAND_VOICE_AND_MESSAGING.md`, which handles what to say. this one
handles how it's typed.

one sentence version: **write like a person who reads a lot, talks fast, punctuates
properly, and undercuts himself before anyone else can.**

---

## 1. registers

there are three. they're all the same voice at different volumes. pick one on
purpose and stay in it. the most common failure is drifting halfway between two.

### informal — default for guides, blog, readmes, social, commit messages

all lowercase, including `i`. apostrophes optional in contractions (`dont`,
`didnt`, `im`, `isnt`). shorthand allowed: `u`, `ur`, `cuz`, `sth`, `ppl`,
`prolly`, `ofc`, `tbh`, `tl;dr`. talks straight at the reader. jokes in
parentheses. emoji: at most one per page, and only when the sentence is already
funny.

> not satisfied with where you are? 😭 well so was i. and i have tried to compile
> all the necessary steps that u might need when planning on transferring unis as
> a ytb student.

> im very into f1. (yeah, we figured, not like the entire site didnt give it away)

> so u wanna make one of those choice of games style interactive stories. good
> choice honestly, theyre super fun to write.

### medium — default for the portfolio, project descriptions, ui copy, essays about work

lowercase headings and labels, sentence case in the body. real apostrophes. no
`u`/`ur` shorthand. still first person, still allowed a joke, but the sentences
are load-bearing. this is the register for anything a stranger will judge you on.

> olympiad training and exams doesn't really teach you tricks. the exam questions
> dont have a pattern. no, it teaches you to stay in the room with a problem long
> after it stops being comfortable.

> I build clean, fast interfaces and small, elegant systems — and the occasional
> browser game.

### formal — applications, letters, translated pages, the formal field guides, anything institutional

full sentence case. every apostrophe. no shorthand, no lowercase `i`, no jokes
that could be misread by someone reading in their second language. still direct,
still concrete, still short sentences. formal here means *unambiguous*, not
*inflated*. the vocabulary doesn't go up, the precision does.

> Universities in Türkiye commonly announce yatay geçiş applications after
> spring-semester grades have been finalized.

> It is one thing when a night you expected would be entirely peaceful is
> disrupted by an unpleasant nightmare. It is another when that nightmare comes
> to life.

### picking one

| you're writing | register |
| --- | --- |
| blog post, field guide, repo readme, changelog, tweet | informal |
| portfolio copy, project blurb, button/label text, cover note for a build | medium |
| scholarship letter, formal guide, legal-ish page, anything translated | formal |

## 2. capitalization

**titles and headings are lowercase.** blog posts, section headings, nav items,
labels, buttons. `about`, `work`, `games`, `box`, `blog`. `sitting with a hard
problem.` `building this page like a pit wall.` no Title Case anywhere, ever.

**capitals are for emphasis, not for decoration.** a capital letter should be
doing a job. proper nouns keep theirs in medium and formal register. in informal
register even those relax. `türkiye`, `english`, `toefl` are fine lowercase, and
inconsistency between one paragraph and the next is not a bug.

**a full-caps word is a legitimate emphasis tool.** one word, rarely, when
italics would be too polite:

> That is NOT the case here.
> without fail, ALL unis have this school.
> unis will always want students (cuz, money, duh.)

**the brand marks are always lowercase**: `sami`, `kmab`, `λ`.

**blog titles take a trailing period.** `sitting with a hard problem.` it reads
as a statement rather than a headline.

## 3. punctuation

punctuation is used, not avoided. this is not a minimalist voice. the marks are
how the timing works.

**semicolons — yes, and correctly.** joining two clauses that are really one
thought.

> I sat on the floor and started crying, not because I got thumped right in the
> face but, because nobody; not even a single soul stood by my side.

> teeth braced at the word I had broken off, just as I like my things: precise.

**ellipsis — the trail-off and the beat.** used to hesitate, to let a thought
drift, or to set up a punchline. `...` and `. . .` both appear; either is fine.

> ...And this is how I spend my time, looking for connections, however obtuse it
> might be.

> Never had I imagined that Nature was this... diverse!

> as with any comparison, the easiest way to determine who is the better option is
> by . . . numbers.

**parentheses — the aside that undercuts the sentence.** this is the single most
characteristic move in the whole voice. the main clause says the thing; the
parenthesis takes the air out of it.

> Who's Sami? (I insist people use Sami over Samuel; too professional)
> He would be concerned that my silence made me unsociable (he would be wrong).
> im very into f1. (yeah, we figured, not like the entire site didnt give it away)
> so when should u start running around like a headless chicken? since the start
> of the year ofc. jk jk.

**em dash — sparingly, for a real interruption.** roughly one every few hundred
words, not one per paragraph. in the essays a plain hyphen often does the same
job (`each chapter closed- every milestone celebrated`) and that's acceptable in
informal drafts. what is *not* acceptable is the em dash as universal glue, which
is the loudest AI tell there is.

**exclamation marks — allowed, when earned.** the voice is enthusiastic. it just
can't be enthusiastic about everything.

> I knew even then that the wastage of any resource was a crime!

**colon — for the setup-payoff.** `so now we know what we are looking for:
kurumlar arası [yurt içi] yatay geçiş.`

**oxford comma — usually, but don't agonize.** the corpus is inconsistent and the
world kept turning.

**dropped apostrophes are a register marker, not an error.** `dont` in a field
guide is correct. `dont` in a scholarship letter is not.

## 4. sentence rhythm

measured across the essay corpus: mean 13.8 words, median 12. one sentence in
eight is under six words. fewer than one in twenty runs past thirty.

the pattern is **short sentence for the hit, longer sentence for the
explanation.** the short one lands; the long one earns it.

> It tasted like poison. My eyes were going back and forth between the glass of
> tomato juice — one sip from full — I was holding in my hand and my mom, who was
> holding a slipper to throw at me if I wasn't going to finish it.

**fragments are deliberate and frequent.** they open sections, they set scenes,
they do the work of a drumbeat.

> Rustling. Silence. Suddenly, gasp.
> Reading and writing. Two sides of a coin.
> Why green?
> And yet, no books were read.

**never let every paragraph be the same length.** a one-line paragraph is a
weapon; three of them in a row is a linkedin post.

## 5. structure

**open with something concrete.** a scene, an object, a number, a blunt claim, or
a question. never with a thesis-shaped abstraction and never with context-setting
about how important the topic is.

> I wonder. Why did Dartmouth name a medical school after a writer?
> There I was, watching through my window the funeral.
> im very into f1.
> not satisfied with where you are? 😭 well so was i.

**questions as section headings, in informal register.** `what is yatay geçiş?`,
`when should u start worrying about this?`, `what do i actually need?`. they read
like the reader asked them, because the reader did.

**tl;dr lines.** in guides, lead a section with `tl;dr:` and the one-sentence
answer, then explain underneath. respect the reader who's in a hurry.

**bold the lead phrase of a list item, not random words mid-sentence.**

> 1. **state what you actually know**, separately from what you assume.
> 2. **find the smallest case that still fails.**

**close by deflating, not by inspiring.** the ending is a joke, an admission, or a
loose end. never a call to action, never a summary of what was just said.

> Turns out, I still have a lot of learning to do.
> This is Sami (not Samuel). And he is not complete yet.
> Now, how can I make her drink this bitter concoction?
> A pressing worry of mine is the dire health portents accompanying writing,
> namely hunched backs, crooked fingers, and huge glasses.
> get ready for a *lot* of blogs.

## 6. word choice

**plain words carry the argument.** technical work gets explained in language a
smart stranger can follow. `a bug you can't explain is just a proof you haven't
finished.`

**big words are jokes or precision, never status.** `betimes`, `indubitably`,
`bamboozled`, `commandeered`, `whisker-less`, `portents`. every one of them
lands inside a gag or a physical scene. an oversized word used straight, to sound
impressive, is the wrong voice.

> At some point, I had so many things I commandeered — with a sweet smile — a
> whole closet to myself.
> It seemed that however whisker-less I was, my curiosity would get me killed.
> And betimes, sarcasm.

**self-correcting escalation is a signature.** say it, then say it harder.

> I have read, nay, eaten, nay, devoured a heap of books.

**numbers, specifics, names.** `6k+ members`, `top 0.01%`, `180% to 14K`,
`three days in the hospital`, `Duny was Emperor Emhyr`. specificity is the
credibility.

**metaphors have to survive being read literally.** racing language is
seasoning: `purple = personal best`, `fastest line`, `box, box`. one per piece,
maximum, and only where it does real work.

---

## 7. dramatize honestly

the voice does dramatize. `it tasted like poison.` `it is another when that
nightmare comes to life.` that's allowed and it's part of the appeal.

the rule is **drama comes from the concrete detail, not from the adjective.** you
earn tension by putting a real moment on the page: the slipper in the mother's
hand, the wolf-shaped bookmark braced at the broken-off word, the ambulance
workers scrambling. you do not earn it by writing `"a truly transformative moment."`

if a sentence would still be dramatic with all its adjectives removed, keep it.
if it collapses, it was decoration.

## 8. what to avoid

<!-- kmab-writing: ignore-start -->

corporate filler, inflated certainty, generic inspiration, performative
humility, unexplained jargon, and metaphors that don't survive literal reading.

and the whole AI register (see `AI_TELLS.md`, the long version). the
short version: no Title Case, no "it's not X, it's Y", no three-adjective triads,
no em dash on every third line, no stacked one-line taglines, no "let's dive in",
no closing call to action, no bullet lists where every bullet is exactly the same
length.

## 9. quick check before publishing

- [ ] register picked on purpose, held all the way through
- [ ] headings and title lowercase
- [ ] opens on something concrete — scene, number, object, or blunt claim
- [ ] at least one short sentence or fragment doing real work
- [ ] sentence lengths vary; paragraph lengths vary
- [ ] semicolons, ellipses, or a parenthetical aside present and used properly
- [ ] em dashes counted — fewer than one per two hundred words
- [ ] every big word is a joke or genuinely the precise one
- [ ] specifics and numbers instead of adjectives
- [ ] ends on a deflation, an admission, or a loose end
- [ ] read `AI_TELLS.md` and confirm none of it is in there
