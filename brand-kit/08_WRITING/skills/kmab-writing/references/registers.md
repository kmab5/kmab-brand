# registers

Three volumes of one voice. All three are genuinely his; none is a "dumbed
down" or "dressed up" version of another. What changes between them is
apostrophes, shorthand, and how much a joke could be misread. What stays
constant: short sentences, concrete openings, real punctuation, no inflation.

## picking one

| you're writing | register |
| --- | --- |
| blog post | informal |
| field guide (informal version) | informal |
| repo README, AGENTS.md, changelog, commit message | informal |
| tweet, telegram post, discord message | informal |
| portfolio section copy, about page | medium |
| project blurb or card description | medium |
| button, label, nav item, empty state, error message | medium |
| cover note attached to a build or a PR description | medium |
| personal essay about work | medium |
| scholarship or application letter | formal |
| field guide (formal version) | formal |
| anything that will be machine-translated | formal |
| anything institutional, legal-adjacent, or read by a committee | formal |

Ambiguous request → default to **medium**.

---

## informal

All lowercase including `i`. Apostrophes optional in contractions. Shorthand
allowed. Direct second-person address. Jokes in parentheses. One emoji per page
maximum.

Shorthand inventory that actually appears: `u`, `ur`, `cuz`, `sth`, `ppl`,
`prolly`, `ofc`, `tbh`, `jk`, `tl;dr`, `PS.`

> not satisfied with where you are? 😭 well so was i. and i have tried to compile
> all the necessary steps that u might need when planning on transferring unis as
> a ytb student. this guide isn't complete yet as i am also attempting my transfer
> while im writing so i'll probably update it as i go.

> so when should u start running around like a headless chicken? since the start
> of the year ofc. jk jk. but seriously, as a uni student, ur major stand out
> point is ur results.

> good news: choicescript is mostly just plain text. u write ur story like normal,
> put commands on lines that start with `*`, and indent the bits that live inside
> choices or conditions.
>
> bad news: choicescript takes indentation *personally*. like, one space out of
> place and it will refuse to speak to u. u have been warned.

> im very into f1. (yeah, we figured, not like the entire site didnt give it away)

Note in the last two: the good news / bad news structure, italics for a single
word doing comic work, and the parenthetical pre-empting the reader's eye-roll.

## medium

Lowercase headings and labels, sentence case in the body. Real apostrophes. No
`u`/`ur`. Still first person, still allowed a joke, but the sentences carry
weight. This is the register for anything a stranger judges you on.

> olympiad training and exams doesn't really teach you tricks. the exam questions
> dont have a pattern. you dont have a data structure or algorithm that you
> learned in DSA class that conveniently fits each question. no, it teaches you to
> stay in the room with a problem long after it stops being comfortable, to keep
> turning it over when the obvious moves are gone.
>
> i've found that that habit turns out to be most of debugging.
>
> a bug you can't explain is just a proof you haven't finished.

> I build clean, fast interfaces and small, elegant systems — and the occasional
> browser game. Olympiad-trained problem solver who likes turning hard ideas into
> things people can actually use.

> A browser rendition of Gebeta with a rules engine, turn logic, and just enough
> AI to make losing interesting.

> Outside the editor I run a 6k-member math community, read too much epic fantasy
> and LitRPG, and lose at table tennis with enthusiasm.

Note: medium tolerates both fully-lowercase body (blog) and sentence-case body
(portfolio). Either is in-register. What it does not tolerate is shorthand or
Title Case headings.

## formal

Full sentence case, complete punctuation, no shorthand, no lowercase `i`.
Formal means unambiguous, not inflated. Vocabulary does not go up.

> If you are not satisfied with your current placement, you are not alone—and you
> may have options. This guide explains the steps involved in transferring
> universities as a YTB scholarship student.

> Universities in Türkiye commonly announce yatay geçiş applications after
> spring-semester grades have been finalized. The main application period is often
> late June, July, or early August.

> It is one thing when a night you expected would be entirely peaceful is
> disrupted by an unpleasant nightmare. It is another when that nightmare comes to
> life.

> It started with excitement, the addictive feeling of adrenaline pumping through
> my veins as I stood in front of my father's old computer. I was playing
> Spider-Man II, swinging through the streets, out-punching an eight-armed Doc Oc.

Note that formal still opens on a concrete scene and still uses short sentences.
The essay register (last two) is the literary end of formal: present-tense
scenes, semicolons, ellipses, dialogue, and a deflating final line are all in
play. What's absent is shorthand and self-interrupting slang, not personality.

### what formal is not

> Applicants are strongly encouraged to leverage all available resources in order
> to facilitate an optimal outcome with respect to their transfer endeavours.

Passive, inflated, three prepositional phrases stacked, `leverage`, `facilitate`,
`optimal`. This is the register the brand actively rejects. Formal register
should read like the informal guide with the slang removed, not like a
different person.

---

## register drift

The most visible failure is a paragraph that starts informal and ends medium, or
a formal document with one stray `dont` in it. Pick, commit, and check the whole
piece at the end.

Legitimate exception: a formal page can carry an informal aside in a callout that
is explicitly marked as the author speaking personally. Anywhere else, hold the
line.
