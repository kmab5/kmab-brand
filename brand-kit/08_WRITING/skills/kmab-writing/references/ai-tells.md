# ai tells

the failure mode this brand cares about most. everything below is something a
language model reliably does and sami reliably doesn't. if a draft contains
three or more of these, throw it out and rewrite from the first concrete image
rather than patching it. patched slop still reads as slop.

---

## structural tells

**Title Case Headings.** the single fastest giveaway. headings here are
lowercase. always.

**the stacked one-line tagline.** three or four fragments on their own lines,
each meant to land like a punch, none of them saying anything:

> This changes everything.
>
> Completely.
>
> And it's only the beginning.

sami's fragments do work. `Rustling. Silence. Suddenly, gasp.` sets a scene.
these do not.

**the antithesis reflex.** "it's not X — it's Y." "this isn't about A. it's
about B." occasionally a real rhetorical move; as a habit, a tic. one per piece
at absolute most, and only when X and Y are genuinely different things.

**the triad.** "fast, clean, and scalable." "clear, concise, and compelling."
adjectives arriving in threes because three sounds complete, not because there
are three things to say. cut to one, or replace with a specific.

**rhetorical question immediately answered.** "so what does this mean? it means
you can ship faster." sami asks real questions and then actually goes and
answers them over four paragraphs (`why did Dartmouth name a medical school
after a writer?`) or leaves them hanging. he doesn't ask-and-immediately-tell.

**the perfectly parallel bullet list.** every bullet the same length, same
grammatical shape, same rhythm. real lists are lumpy. some items need a clause,
some need a sentence, one needs a parenthetical.

**every paragraph is three sentences.** vary it or it reads like a template.

**the closing call to action.** `"so what are you waiting for? go build
something."` endings here deflate. they don't recruit.

**the summary paragraph that repeats what was just said.** if the reader needed
the recap, the piece was too long.

## punctuation tells

**the em dash as universal glue.** one every second or third sentence, replacing
commas, colons, parentheses, and full stops indiscriminately. the corpus has
about six em dashes across ninety thousand characters of essays. use them like
they cost money.

**bold scattered across half-sentences for "punch."** bolding here goes on the
lead phrase of a list item, not on random emphatic clauses mid-paragraph.

**emoji bullets.** 🚀 ✨ 🔥 as list markers. one emoji per page, inside a
sentence, when the sentence is already funny. never as decoration.

**scare quotes around a term the writer just invented.** if it needs quotes it
needs a definition.

**no semicolons, no ellipses, no parenthetical asides.** the absence is itself a
tell. this voice punctuates. a page with nothing but commas and em dashes isn't
this voice.

## vocabulary tells

the following words are not banned outright, since some have honest uses, but each
one in a draft should be individually defended:

delve · leverage · robust · seamless · seamlessly · elevate · unlock · harness ·
landscape (figurative) · realm · tapestry · testament to · at its core ·
navigate (figurative) · foster · underscore · pivotal · crucial · myriad
(as filler; sami uses it, but literally) · nuanced · holistic · game-changer ·
transformative · cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · best-in-class · ecosystem ·
synergy · streamline · empower · curated · bespoke · meticulously crafted

and these openers, all of which are dead on arrival:

- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "in an era where"
- "let's dive in" / "let's unpack this"
- "here's the thing"
- "the result?"
- "but here's where it gets interesting"
- "it's worth noting that"
- "that said," used as a paragraph opener twice in one piece

<!-- kmab-writing: ignore-end -->

**the difference that matters.** sami uses big words too: `betimes`,
`indubitably`, `bamboozled`, `whisker-less`. the difference is that his are
jokes or physical precision, sitting inside a scene. AI's are decoration,
sitting where a plain word would have worked better. before keeping a big word,
ask: is this funny, or is this exact? if neither, cut it.

## tonal tells

**inflated certainty.** "this is the definitive guide." sami's guides say `not
really confirmed whether my info here is accurate or not so take with a pinch of
salt` and `i have yet to prove this is possible yet.`

**generic inspiration.** "believe in yourself and the rest will follow." the
corpus does earnestness (`i want to be the hope for the hopeless`), but it's
earned by three paragraphs of a specific dying woman in a specific hospital
corridor first.

**performative humility.** "i'm no expert, but…" as a throat-clear. real
uncertainty gets stated plainly and specifically instead.

**the enthusiasm floor.** AI is uniformly, mildly positive about everything. this
voice is allowed to find things annoying (`choicescript takes indentation
*personally*`) and allowed to be flat when flat is accurate.

**explaining the obvious to sound thorough.** if the reader knows it, skip it.

---

## the smell test

read the draft out loud. if it sounds like a competent stranger presenting to a
room, it's wrong. it should sound like someone who's read too much fantasy,
talks fast, is slightly embarrassed about how much he cares, and would rather
make a joke at his own expense than let a sentence get too pleased with itself.
