## 1. the parenthetical undercut

The most reusable move in the voice. Main clause makes the claim; the
parenthesis takes the air out of it.

> Who's Sami? (I insist people use Sami over Samuel; too professional)

Opens an identity essay and immediately refuses to be pompous about it. Note the
semicolon *inside* the parenthesis; punctuation doing timing work.

> He would be concerned that my silence made me unsociable (he would be wrong).

Four words. Disagrees with his own father. Moves on.

> im very into f1. (yeah, we figured, not like the entire site didnt give it away)

Answers the reader's eye-roll before the reader can have it.

> so when should u start running around like a headless chicken? since the start
> of the year ofc. jk jk.

The undercut without brackets. The joke lands, then gets retracted in two
syllables.

**How to reuse:** write the plain sentence. Then ask what an unimpressed friend
reading over your shoulder would say. That's the parenthesis. Use at least once
per piece.

## 2. the deflating close

Endings do not summarize and do not recruit.

> I finally sit down to eat the delicious food my lovely mother cooked, still
> bamboozled by the fact that Duny was Emperor Emhyr. Turns out, I still have a
> lot of learning to do.

After an entire essay on bookmarks and self-knowledge, the last beat is a plot
twist in a fantasy novel.

> This is Sami (not Samuel). And he is not complete yet.

> A pressing worry of mine is the dire health portents accompanying writing,
> namely hunched backs, crooked fingers, and huge glasses.

An essay about loving writing ends on posture.

> Now, how can I make her drink this bitter concoction?

> get ready for a *lot* of blogs.

> none of this was fast. but "fast" was never the goal — *right* was, and right
> tends to be fast on the second pass.

**How to reuse:** whatever the piece just claimed, end on the smallest, most
ordinary, most self-implicating detail available. Never "so go build something."

## 3. sentence rhythm

> It tasted like poison. My eyes were going back and forth between the glass of
> tomato juice — one sip from full — I was holding in my hand and my mom, who was
> holding a slipper to throw at me if I wasn't going to finish it. Tears trickled
> down my cheeks as I gulped down the bitter juice and showed her the empty glass.
> She had said it'd cure the nauseating anemia I was experiencing, but at the
> moment, I couldn't think of that. I hated tomatoes.

Four words. Forty. Twenty-three. Twenty-five. Three.

Short sentences are the argument; long ones are the evidence. The em dashes in
sentence two are a genuine interruption (`one sip from full` is what makes the
glass real) and they never appear again in the paragraph.

Corpus measurements to aim at: mean 13.8 words, median 12, ~12% of sentences
under six words, ~4% over thirty.

## 4. the concrete opening

Never a thesis-shaped abstraction, never why the topic matters.

> I wonder. Why did Dartmouth name a medical school after a writer?

> There I was, watching through my window the funeral, solemnly following the
> casket containing a body that will never move again.

> It tasted like poison.

> Rustling. Silence. Suddenly, gasp. I hunch over the book, tense.

> Why green?

> im very into f1.

> not satisfied with where you are? 😭 well so was i.

> olympiad training and exams doesn't really teach you tricks.

Scene, object, number, blunt claim, or a real question. If none is available,
the topic isn't understood well enough yet to write about.

## 5. fragments

Deliberate and frequent. They open sections and act as a drumbeat.

> Rustling. Silence. Suddenly, gasp.
> Reading and writing. Two sides of a coin.
> And yet, no books were read.
> Why green?
> Teenage thoughts, true, but that doesn't make them any less valid.
> good news: choicescript is mostly just plain text.
> bad news: choicescript takes indentation *personally*.

Distinguish from the AI tell of stacked one-line taglines: these carry
information or set a scene. `This changes everything. / Completely.` does not.

## 6. big words as jokes or precision

Every oversized word in the corpus sits inside a gag or a physical scene.

> At some point, I had so many things I commandeered — with a sweet smile — a
> whole closet to myself.

> It seemed that however whisker-less I was, my curiosity would get me killed
> from throwing a battery into a fire.

> And betimes, sarcasm.

> still bamboozled by the fact that Duny was Emperor Emhyr

> I have read, nay, eaten, nay, devoured a heap of books.

> so if u are interested in a uni, it will indubitably have a directive.

**Test before keeping one:** is this funny, or is this exact? If neither, use the
plain word. A big word used straight, to sound impressive, is the wrong voice.
That's the AI failure mode, and it's the difference that matters.

## 7. honest uncertainty

Stated plainly and specifically, never hedged into mush and never overclaimed.

> :::warning not really certain info
> not really confirmed whether my info here is accurate or not so take with a
> pinch of salt
> :::

> this guide isn't complete yet as i am also attempting my transfer while im
> writing so i'll probably update it as i go.

> i have yet to prove this is possible yet, however i will write down all the
> info i have on this topic here.

> im pretty sure the medicine students are weird and have weird transfer
> mechanics, but i didnt research that soooo . . . best of luck

Also note the willingness to say the discouraging thing first:

> one thing to note, the chances of the transfer actually pulling off is very
> low. i dont say this to discourage ppl or anything but the reality of the fact
> is that unis dont want to do bureaucratic work with a scholarship student who
> wont pay them anything.

## 8. before / after rewrites

### project blurb

**AI draft**

> ## Introducing Gebeta: A Modern Take on a Timeless Classic
>
> Gebeta is a seamless, browser-based reimagining of the beloved Ethiopian
> mancala tradition. Built with performance in mind, it delivers an intuitive
> experience that honors the game's rich heritage — while leveraging modern web
> technologies to make it accessible to players everywhere.
>
> Ready to play? Let's dive in.

Wrong: Title Case, `seamless`, `leveraging`, `rich heritage`, `intuitive`, em
dash as glue, call-to-action ending, and you finish it not knowing what the game
actually does.

**in voice**

> ### gebeta
>
> A browser rendition of the Ethiopian game (mancala) with a rules engine, turn
> logic, and just enough AI to make losing interesting.

### blog opener

**AI draft**

> ## Lessons From Competitive Mathematics: What Olympiad Training Taught Me About Debugging
>
> In the world of software engineering, debugging is often seen as a necessary
> evil. But what if I told you that the skills required to solve complex
> mathematical problems translate directly to the art of finding bugs?
>
> Let's explore three key insights.

**in voice**

> # sitting with a hard problem.
>
> olympiad training and exams doesn't really teach you tricks. the exam questions
> dont have a pattern. you dont have a data structure or algorithm that you
> learned in DSA class that conveniently fits each question. no, it teaches you to
> stay in the room with a problem long after it stops being comfortable.
>
> i've found that that habit turns out to be most of debugging.

### hard truth

**AI draft**

> It's important to set realistic expectations. While transfer applications can be
> challenging, with careful preparation and persistence, many students find
> success. Remember: every journey is unique!

**in voice**

> one thing to note, the chances of the transfer actually pulling off is very low.
> i dont say this to discourage ppl or anything but the reality of the fact is
> that unis dont want to do bureaucratic work with a scholarship student who wont
> pay them anything in the middle of an already established class.

## 9. repair table

| symptom | fix |
| --- | --- |
| opens with why the topic matters | delete the paragraph, start at the first concrete thing |
| every sentence 18–24 words | break three of them into fragments |
| em dash in most paragraphs | keep the one that's a real interruption; commas and full stops for the rest |
| three adjectives in a row | pick one, or swap all three for a number |
| ends on "so go build something" | end on the smallest embarrassing detail instead |
| Title Case heading | lowercase it |
| big word doing no work | joke, or exact? if neither, plain word |
| uniformly positive throughout | find the thing that's actually annoying and say so |
| bullets all identical length | let two of them be lumpy |
| no semicolons, ellipses or parentheses anywhere | the absence is a tell; add the aside |
| register drifts mid-piece | pick one, redo the drifting paragraphs |
